PHASE 2 FINDINGS: OUT-OF-SAMPLE EVALUATION OF logHAR FORECASTS OF BTC RV
=========================================================================
Run 2026-10-26. Code: phase2_oos.py (+ har.py), input data/rv_daily_tau0.parquet.
Regenerate the tables in section 4 with:  python3 phase2_oos.py
Section 6 comes from compute_rv.py (needs the 1-minute data).


1. DESIGN (fixed 2026-10-26, before any results; see ts_project_plan.md)
------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Target: next-day log RV (log of daily realized volatility, 5-min returns, day from 00:00 UTC).
- Test: 1,003 one-day-ahead forecasts, origins t = 2024-01-01 .. 2026-09-29,
  targets t+1 = 2024-01-02 .. 2026-09-30. Same test days for every model.
- Refit at every origin on rows whose target is known at t (rows s <= t-1).
- Window: rolling, last 700 usable rows (main); expanding (robustness).
  Training rows at the first origin: 700 for models 1-4, 640 for model 5, 365 for model 6
  (the 90- and 365-day averages need more history: a handicap for models 5 and 6).
- Models:
    1 random walk         tomorrow = today
    2 seasonal naive      the target's weekday one week earlier (day t-6)
    3 HAR (d, w, m)            plain HAR, horizons 1, 7, 30 days
    4 HAR (d, w, m) + DoW      + target-day weekday dummies (Monday baseline)  <- carried forward
    5 HAR (d, w, m, q) + DoW   + quarterly average, 90 days (rv_q)
    6 HAR (d, w, m, y) + DoW   + yearly average, 365 days (rv_y)
  Regressors: rv_d, rv_w, rv_m, rv_q, rv_y. The names give the horizon, not a mechanism.
  The 90-day term was added for a statistical reason: it is the next rung on the
  1 -> 7 -> 30 ladder of timescales, and the weekday-adjusted ACF is still about 0.14 at
  lags 50-70. A behavioural link (quarterly futures expiries on CME and the major
  exchanges) is possible, but it was not the motivation and was not tested. The 365-day
  term is a drift check: a yearly average acts like a slowly moving intercept, a
  competitor to the rolling window.
- Losses: MSE on log RV (primary); QLIKE on variance, mean of s2/h - log(s2/h) - 1
  (secondary); MAE and MSE on variance (supplementary).
- Variance forecast: h = exp(2 yhat) x smearing factor (mean of exp(2u) over training
  residuals). The Gaussian factor exp(2 s^2) is reported as a diagnostic.
- Diebold-Mariano: d = loss(A) - loss(B), Newey-West variance (Bartlett, 6 lags by the
  rule floor(4 (n/100)^(2/9)) with n = 1,003), two-sided normal p-values.

Choices made where the design left room (decided before the run; to confirm):
- "Test period" read as forecast origins, so the last target is 2026-09-30.
- Benchmarks (1, 2) get their smearing factor from their own errors on the same 700
  training rows as the HAR models.
- "Levels" losses computed on the variance scale (s2 vs h).


2. MAIN FINDINGS (rolling window)
---------------------------------
1. The weekday dummies help out of sample, by a lot. HAR (d, w, m) + DoW vs HAR (d, w, m):
   MSE on log RV 0.0746 vs 0.1354 (-45%), DM -12.50, p < 0.001;
   QLIKE 0.1918 vs 0.2741 (-30%), DM -7.64, p < 0.001.
   The in-sample gain (R^2 0.43 -> 0.65) carries over to forecasting.
2. Model 4 beats both naive benchmarks easily: MSE 0.40x the random walk's, QLIKE 0.52x.
   Model 2 vs model 4: DM +8.94 (MSE), +6.73 (QLIKE).
3. Longer horizons add nothing. Model 4 vs 5 (+ rv_q): DM -1.14 (MSE), -0.46 (QLIKE);
   model 4 vs 6 (+ rv_y): DM -1.49, -1.05. Both point estimates slightly favour model 4.
   With the 700-day rolling window, a slow-moving term is not needed. So the quarterly
   term gives no support to a quarterly (futures-expiry) effect either.
4. Seasonal naive vs random walk: a tie on MSE (DM +0.25, p 0.80), but the seasonal naive
   is worse on QLIKE (DM -1.92, p 0.055, i.e. the random walk is better). Copying last
   week's same weekday captures the season but throws away the persistence.
5. Rolling vs expanding: almost identical (model 4 MSE 0.0746 vs 0.0750, QLIKE 0.1918 vs
   0.1924). Every conclusion above holds under the expanding window.
6. Back-transform diagnostic: the Gaussian factor gives slightly lower QLIKE than smearing
   for every model (model 4: 0.1884 vs 0.1918). The mean factors are 1.30 (smearing) vs
   1.19 (Gaussian), so smearing gives larger variance forecasts. A sentence for the
   write-up, not a design change.
7. Rolling coefficient paths of HAR (d, w, m) + DoW (700-row fits, section 5):
   - beta_d dips to 0.39 (Dec 2024), then rises to 0.55 by Sep 2026.
   - beta_w drifts down, 0.34 -> 0.25; beta_m stays small, 0.03 to 0.16, its band
     always covering zero.
   - Saturday deepens, -0.72 -> -0.91, outside the earlier +-2 SE band.
   - Sunday falls to -0.40 (Feb 2025), then recovers to -0.24.
   - Weekday shifts move more than the HAR betas, which supports the rolling window.


3. NOTES
--------
- The out-of-sample MSE of model 4 (0.075) is below the full-sample in-sample residual
  variance (0.293^2 = 0.086). This is not a red flag: 2024-26 is calmer (per-year
  in-sample residual sd 0.27, 0.28, 0.26 vs 0.33 in 2023).
- Look-ahead check: replacing every day after an origin with random values leaves all
  forecasts made at that origin unchanged.
